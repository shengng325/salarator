Exchange rate & how this is calculated

  • We compare 12 months of salary, without bonuses or other income, and assume you’re a tax resident in each country. Salaries are converted at the rate above before tax is calculated. Changing the input currency keeps the number you entered. Tax reliefs stay in the currency shown beside them.
  • For Singapore, we assume you’re 55 or below and pay full CPF rates: 20% from you and 17% from your employer, on monthly salary up to S$8,000 in 2026. Lower salaries follow CPF’s reduced contribution rules. Tax uses resident rates from YA 2024 onwards, with relief for your CPF contributions and S$1,000 of earned income. Total personal relief is capped at S$80,000. One-off tax rebates aren’t included.
  • For Malaysia, we assume you’re a Malaysian citizen under 60. EPF is estimated at 11% from you and 13% from your employer for salaries up to RM5,000, or 12% above that. Your payslip may differ slightly because KWSP uses contribution bands and rounding.
  • Malaysia tax uses YA 2025 rates. We include RM9,000 personal relief and up to RM4,000 EPF relief. Progressive tax also includes the RM400 rebate if your chargeable income is RM35,000 or less. Only enter other reliefs in the extra relief fields.
  • The 15% REP option applies after reliefs. It assumes you’ve been approved for the programme and are still within its eligible period.
  • Take-home pay is salary minus tax and your CPF/EPF contribution. Total pay after tax adds both your contribution and your employer’s back in. SOCSO, EIS, insurance, zakat and other deductions or benefits aren’t included.
  • Monthly tax is the yearly estimate divided by 12, so it may differ from payroll deductions. The difference column shows which amount is higher, with the percentage measured against the lower amount. If the lower amount is zero, there’s no percentage to show.
